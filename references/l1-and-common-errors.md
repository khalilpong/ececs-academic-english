# Common errors and watch lists

These are frequent patterns to check, not assumptions about any writer. Correct them where they occur and explain the rule briefly when the user wants to learn.

## Frequent learner errors (many first languages)

- **Articles:** missing or extra *a/an/the* ("The experiment shows that ~~the~~ temperature affects…"; "We designed ~~a~~ new system" → "a new system").
- **Uncountable nouns:** *evidence, research, equipment, information, feedback, software, knowledge* take no plural *-s* and no *a*. Use *a piece of*, *studies*, *items of equipment* instead.
- **Subject–verb agreement:** especially with long subjects ("The results of the third test *show*…", "*Data* is/are…" according to the style used).
- **Tense consistency:** method in past tense, established facts in present tense, plans in future tense; do not drift within a paragraph.
- **Comma splices and run-on sentences:** "The load increased, the beam deflected" → use a full stop, a semicolon or a linking word.
- **Word form:** *analysis/analyse/analytical*, *significance/significant/significantly*.
- **Prepositions:** *depend on, consist of, focus on, compared with/to, in terms of*.

## Patterns common in Chinese-L1 writing

- **Calques of common openings:** "With the development of technology…", "Nowadays, more and more…", "As we all know…". Usually replace them with a specific statement of the topic.
- **Topic–comment structures:** "This method, its accuracy is high" → "This method is highly accurate."
- **Overuse of *very*, *so*, *a lot of*, *really*:** replace with precise intensifiers or numbers.
- **Missing plurals and articles** after numbers or with general nouns: "three sample" → "three samples".
- **Long noun stacks:** "power system stability analysis method improvement" → "an improved method for analysing power-system stability".
- **Over-literal formality:** *utilise* for *use*, *commence* for *start* where the simpler word is precise.
- **Logical connectors used for rhythm** (*besides*, *what's more*, *in a word*): keep only where they express a real relationship, and prefer academic forms (*in addition*, *in short*).

## Watch list of often-empty phrases

Use judgement: these are not banned, but they are often vague, inflated or unnecessary in academic writing.

| Phrase | Often better |
| --- | --- |
| play an important / vital role in | name the actual effect: *reduces*, *controls*, *determines* |
| in today's world / in recent years | a specific time or context, or delete |
| it is worth noting that | delete, or state why it matters |
| a wide range of | state the range or give examples |
| delve into, navigate, landscape, realm, tapestry | *examine*, *manage*, *field*, *area*, or delete |
| groundbreaking, cutting-edge, revolutionary | describe the specific advance and its evidence |
| in conclusion, it can be seen that | state the conclusion directly |
| has many advantages | name the advantages |

The test is always: does the phrase add precise meaning here? If not, cut or replace it.
