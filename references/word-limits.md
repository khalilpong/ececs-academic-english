# Working to a word limit

## First, establish the rules

- What is the limit, and is it a hard maximum or a guide (for example ±10%)?
- What counts towards it: headings, in-text citations, tables, captions, footnotes, appendices? What does not (often the reference list)?
- Are there per-question or per-section allocations, for example in proportion to marks?
- If any of this is unclear, say so and report the count both ways.

## What must survive trimming

- Every part of the task the brief requires (each question, sub-question or required section).
- Conditions, caveats, limitations and the strength of claims.
- Citations that support retained claims.
- Numbers, units and technical terms.
- Statements of missing information or assumptions that the argument depends on.

## Trimming techniques, from least to most invasive

1. **Remove framing:** "It is important to note that", "In this section, we will discuss", "As mentioned above".
2. **Remove repetition:** points already made, restated conclusions, duplicated examples.
3. **Tighten wording:** "in order to" → "to"; "due to the fact that" → "because"; "a total of 12" → "12"; "carry out an evaluation of" → "evaluate".
4. **Merge sentences** that share a subject or make one point.
5. **Shorten repeated citations** where the style allows (for example a page or slide number after a full citation in the same paragraph, if the course permits).
6. **Use a compact table** for parallel comparisons, if the brief allows and tables count the same.
7. **Cut lower-value content**, and only with the user's agreement: secondary examples, background the marker already knows.

Never move counted text into tables, footnotes or images to "hide" words, unless the brief says those are excluded.

## Reporting

Give the count before and after, per section when allocations apply, and list anything you removed that changes content rather than wording, so the user can decide whether to restore it.
