# Provenance

This is a user-made writing skill, not an official university, IELTS or institutional standard.

## Origin

Version 1 (2026-10-19) was built from one student's coursework language brief (ECECS Pro). The brief asked for undergraduate assessment writing at an English proficiency of IELTS 6.5–7.5, in an academic style without a mechanical or AI-generated tone. It pointed to English for Academic Purposes resources such as [UEfAP](https://www.uefap.org/).

That brief did not specify mandatory British spelling, sentence-length limits, banned words, a citation system or a guaranteed IELTS result. The skill therefore treats the proficiency range as a practical complexity target and "natural tone" as clear, specific prose, not as a claim about authorship or AI detection.

## Defaults versus requirements

These are editorial defaults that any brief or user can override:

- British spelling when the text and brief establish no variety.
- IELTS 6.5–7.5 / CEFR C1 complexity for undergraduate work.
- Light polishing rather than rewriting.
- Explanations in the language the user writes in.

Course-specific rules (citation style, platform, page or word limits, team rules) belong to each assignment's brief and are intentionally not built into the skill.

## Version 2 (generalised)

Version 2 generalises the skill for written assignments and reports in any course and discipline, and for any LLM or agent. The new reference files (assignment types, disciplines, citations, hedging, word limits, common errors, examples) are written guidance drawing on general academic-writing practice. They are not quotations from any institution's guide. Institutions' own guides always take precedence.

## Optional reading

- [UEfAP: Features of academic writing](https://www.uefap.org/writing-features-introduction/): precision, explicitness, hedging, responsibility and organisation. Use it to improve clarity, not to maximise formality.
