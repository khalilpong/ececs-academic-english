# Hedging and claim strength

Match each claim's strength to its evidence. Editing should not change a claim's strength unless the user asks; if a claim looks too strong or too weak for its evidence, flag it.

## Certainty ladder

| Strength | Typical wording | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certain | *is, does, will, must* | Established facts, definitions, requirements, measured results |
| Strong | *clearly indicates, shows, is very likely* | Consistent evidence from reliable data |
| Moderate | *suggests, indicates, is likely, tends to* | Reasonable inference from limited evidence |
| Tentative | *may, might, could, appears to* | Possibility, hypothesis, early or indirect evidence |
| Speculative | *it is possible that, one explanation could be* | Ideas with little direct support |

Obligation is different from probability. *Must* (a requirement) should not become *may* (a possibility), and *should* (a recommendation) should not become *will* (a prediction).

## Reporting verbs

| Neutral | Tentative | Strong | Disagreeing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| states, describes, reports, notes | suggests, proposes, speculates | shows, demonstrates, establishes | claims, questions, disputes |

Choose the verb that matches what the source actually does. *Smith (2020) demonstrates* is stronger than *Smith (2020) suggests*; do not upgrade or downgrade a source's position.

## Common problems

- **Over-claiming:** "This proves the design is optimal" from one test. Suggest "This suggests the design performs well under the tested conditions."
- **Over-hedging:** "It could perhaps be possible that the results may indicate…" Suggest "The results may indicate…"
- **Lost conditions:** "Revenue would be £6,000, assuming 200 users" must not become "Revenue will be £6,000."
- **Estimate becomes fact:** "Bench tests suggest a three-year life" must not become "The product lasts three years."
- **Plan becomes result:** "We will test the prototype" must not become "We tested the prototype."
