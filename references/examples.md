# Examples

All examples are invented for instruction; they are not research evidence.

## Light polish

**Engineering (lab report)**
- Original: "The purpose of this test is to carry out an evaluation of the sensor reliability, and the result is very good."
- Revised: "This test evaluates sensor reliability; the sensor met all five accuracy thresholds."
- Note: the second clause replaces "very good" with the evidence. If the text gives no evidence, keep the claim and flag it instead: *"very good" is unsupported; add the measured result.*

**Business (case study)**
- Original: "Nowadays, more and more companies use social media, so the company should also do it to get more customers."
- Revised: "Because the firm's main competitors already recruit customers through social media, it should test a targeted Instagram campaign to attract new buyers."
- Note: the generic opening became a specific reason from the case. Only make this kind of change if the case actually contains that reason; otherwise flag the gap.

**Humanities (essay)**
- Original: "This poem talks about loss and it uses a lot of images of winter."
- Revised: "The poem explores loss, using recurring winter imagery to suggest emotional numbness."
- Note: "suggest" keeps the interpretation tentative; the reading itself is the author's.

## Preserving claim strength

- Planned work: "This test will evaluate sensor reliability" must not become "This test evaluated…".
- Uncertainty: "The proposed system could reduce review time" must not become "The system reduces review time" without evidence.
- Obligation: "The controller must retain the log after power loss" must not become "may retain".
- Conditions: "Assuming 200 paying users, annual revenue would be £6,000" must not become "Annual revenue will be £6,000".
- Unsupported comparison: flag "This is the most reliable design available"; a language edit cannot establish the comparison.

## Trimming without losing content

- Original (39 words): "It is important to note that, in order to ensure the safety of users, the team carried out a total of three separate tests on the prototype, which were all conducted in the laboratory under controlled conditions (Lee, 2023)."
- Trimmed (19 words): "To ensure user safety, the team tested the prototype three times in the laboratory under controlled conditions (Lee, 2023)."
- Kept: the purpose, the number of tests, the setting and the citation.

## Group report consistency

- Section A: "We utilised a PID controller…"; Section B: "The controller used was proportional–integral–derivative (PID)…"; Section C: "the P.I.D. controller".
- Consistent version: define "proportional–integral–derivative (PID) controller" at first use, then use "PID controller" throughout, with one voice (for example "we") as the team has agreed.

## An over-edit to avoid

- Original: "Bench tests suggest that the battery may last about three years, although only four units were tested."
- Over-edited (wrong): "Extensive testing has demonstrated that the battery delivers a robust three-year lifespan."
- Problems: "suggest" became "demonstrated"; the estimate became a fact; the four-unit limitation disappeared; "extensive" and "robust" are unsupported.
- Acceptable polish: "Bench tests on four units suggest a battery life of about three years."
