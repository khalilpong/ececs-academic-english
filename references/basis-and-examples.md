# Basis and examples

Prepared 2026-10-19. This is a user-derived writing skill, not an official ECECS or university policy.

## Primary project instruction

The local source `ECECS_Pro/Instruction.md` was read directly. It specifies undergraduate assessment/coursework, English proficiency corresponding to IELTS 6.5–7.5, an academic style without a mechanical or AI-generated tone, and optional consultation of English for Academic Purposes resources, pointing to https://www.uefap.org/.

The original does not specify mandatory British spelling, a sentence-length limit, banned words, a citation system, or a guaranteed IELTS result. The skill interprets the proficiency range as a practical complexity target and the tone requirement as clear, natural expression, not an authorship or detection claim.

## Illustrative editing decisions

These examples are invented for instruction, not research evidence.

- Redundant phrasing: “The purpose of this test is to carry out an evaluation of sensor reliability.” → “This test evaluates sensor reliability.” Preserve planned tense if it is a future test: “This test will evaluate sensor reliability.”
- Preserve uncertainty: “The proposed system could reduce review time.” must not become “The system reduces review time” unless the author supplies evidence for that stronger claim.
- Preserve obligation: “The controller must retain the log after power loss.” must not become “The controller may retain the log after power loss.”
- Preserve the conditional model: “Assuming 200 paying users, annual revenue would be £6,000.” must not become “Annual revenue will be £6,000.”
- Unsupported claim: flag “This is the most reliable design available” for evidence or author reconsideration. A stylistic edit alone cannot establish the comparison.
