# Citations and mechanics

## Citations: preserve, check, never invent

- Keep the citation style the text uses (for example Harvard, APA, IEEE, MLA, Chicago, OSCOLA, Vancouver). Convert between styles only when asked, and then convert every instance consistently.
- Never add, remove, invent or "complete" a source. If a claim seems to need support, add a content flag instead.
- Check, and report rather than fix, any mismatch:
  - in-text citations without a reference-list entry, and entries never cited;
  - author or year differences between the text and the list;
  - direct quotations without page numbers where the style requires them.
- When rephrasing a sentence, keep each citation attached to the claim it supports.
- Secondary citations ("cited in") and lecture or course-material citations follow the style the course specifies. If none is given, keep the user's format.
- In-text citations usually count towards word limits; the reference list usually does not. Check the brief.

## Quick reference: in-text forms

| Style | In-text form | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harvard | (Smith, 2020, p. 4) | Many UK courses |
| APA 7 | (Smith, 2020, p. 4) | Psychology, education, social sciences |
| IEEE | [3] | Engineering, computing |
| MLA 9 | (Smith 4) | Literature, languages |
| Chicago notes | Footnote number¹ | History, arts |
| OSCOLA | Footnote number¹ | UK law |
| Vancouver | (3) or superscript³ | Medicine, health |

Details vary between institutions. The course's own guide always wins.

## Numbers and units

- Keep values, units and precision exactly as given.
- Put a space between a number and its unit (5 kg, 230 V, 80 °C), except for %, ° for angles and similar exceptions in the style used.
- Use an en dash for ranges (10–20 Hz) and be consistent.
- Do not convert units or round numbers unless asked.
- Many styles spell out numbers below ten in prose but use numerals with units. Follow the style used in the text.

## Abbreviations and terms

- Define an abbreviation at first use, then use it consistently.
- Do not introduce abbreviations the author did not use, except where the brief requires them.
- Use one term for one concept throughout; do not vary technical terms for "elegance".

## Spelling variety

- Keep the variety already used (British, American or another). If there is none and nothing is specified, use British spelling.
- Be consistent across text, headings, captions and tables, including the choice between -ise and -ize in British English.
- Do not change spellings inside quotations, titles of works, proper names or code.

## Formatting

- Preserve headings, numbering, lists, tables, captions and cross-references.
- Keep any formatting the template requires (fonts, spacing, placeholders to be replaced). If an edit would affect a required format, say so.
