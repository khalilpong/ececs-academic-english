# Level descriptors

The default target is clear undergraduate academic prose at about **IELTS 6.5–7.5 (CEFR C1)**. This is a practical complexity target, not a predicted score. The aim is writing that a lecturer reads easily and trusts, not the most complex English possible.

## What the target looks like

| Feature | Too simple (≈ B1–B2) | On target (≈ C1) | Inflated (avoid) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vocabulary | General words: *big, good, show, get* | Precise academic and discipline words: *substantial, reliable, indicate, obtain* | Rare or showy words: *prodigious, efficacious, elucidate* where simpler words work |
| Collocation | *do an experiment, make a research* | *conduct an experiment, carry out research* | *effectuate an empirical undertaking* |
| Sentences | Mostly short, simple sentences; repeated *and/so/but* | Mix of simple and complex sentences; relative clauses, conditionals, passive where useful | Long chains of clauses that hide the main point |
| Cohesion | *Firstly, Secondly, Lastly* in every paragraph | Links that show real relationships: *because, however, as a result, this suggests* | Stacked connectors: *Moreover, furthermore, additionally, it is also worth noting that* |
| Stance | Absolute claims: *This proves…* | Calibrated claims: *This suggests…, The data indicate…* | Hedges on everything: *It could perhaps possibly be argued…* |
| Noun use | Repeated pronouns, vague *it/this/thing* | Clear referents; moderate noun phrases | Heavy nominalisation: *the implementation of the optimisation of the reduction of…* |

## The same sentence at three levels

- **Too simple:** "We did a test and the new design was better because it got less errors."
- **On target:** "In testing, the new design produced fewer errors than the baseline, which suggests that the revised filter is more robust."
- **Inflated:** "The empirical evaluation conclusively demonstrated the unequivocal superiority of the novel architecture in terms of error minimisation."

## Adjusting the target

- **CEFR B2 / IELTS 6.0:** shorter sentences, more common vocabulary, explicit links. Use for foundation-year work or when the user asks for simpler English.
- **Postgraduate / C1+:** denser argument, more precise hedging and evaluation, more discipline-specific terms. Clarity still comes first.

Whatever the level, precision beats sophistication: a correct technical term is always preferred to an impressive-sounding general word.
